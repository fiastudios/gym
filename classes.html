Riverside athletic club

Forty-three classes. One week.

Capped rooms, named coaches, and a clock that ends when we said it would. Hold a spot, or join the waitlist if the hour is full.

The board

This week at the club.

Strength

Barbell work, taught properly.

Foundations is for anyone new to a bar, and for anyone coming back after a long break. The intermediate hour assumes you can squat, hinge, and press with a coach in the room, not on your shoulder. Both are programmed by Maya and capped at twelve.

  • 50–70 minutes
  • All levels & intermediate
  • Cap of 12
  • Belts and blocks provided

Conditioning

Hard enough. Not a hangover.

Jordan builds intervals, carries, and mixed pieces you can repeat the next day. Engine is the sharper hour. The all-levels class scales the load, not your dignity. Everything finishes when the board says it finishes.

  • 30–40 minutes
  • All levels & intermediate
  • Cap of 16
  • Kettlebells to 32 kg

Boxing

Footwork first.

Leo teaches boxing as a skill. Guard, feet, and bag work. There is no sparring in the regular class. Saturday has an invitational hour for people who have asked for it. Most members never do, and still leave fitter than they expected.

  • 45–50 minutes
  • All levels
  • Cap of 14
  • Wraps and gloves to borrow

Ride

Same coaching. Darker room.

Twenty-eight bikes, a plan on the board before you clip in, and resistance you choose. Easy Ride on Sundays is the aerobic hour. The weekday rides are intervals. Nobody is shouted at.

  • 40–45 minutes
  • All levels
  • 28 bikes
  • Shoes to borrow

Mobility

The hour that keeps the others possible.

Amira's sessions are hips, shoulders, and breathing. Come if you sit all day. Come if you already lift four days a week. Sunday's longer hour adds a quiet finish. Mats are on the rack. You do not need to be flexible to start.

  • 40–50 minutes
  • All levels
  • Cap of 14
  • Mats provided
